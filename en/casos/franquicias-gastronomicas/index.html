Context

Three Chilean restaurant brands wanted to grow by selling franchises: Club Gordos, a street-food style bar (minimum investment CLP 150M); GRDS, a smash burger joint; and Fast no Junk, healthy fast food (both around CLP 75M minimum).

The problem

The target audience is tiny: people with tens of millions of pesos ready to invest. On social media most of the interest comes from curious people with no capital, and the sales team needed meetings with real investors, not volume.

What I did

  • Meta instant forms with qualifying questions: previous franchise experience and how much the person wanted to invest.
  • Creative angle focused on joining a proven franchise with a high success rate, not on "starting a business".
  • Zapier automation: every lead landed instantly in a spreadsheet so the sales team could reach out fast.
  • An international expansion test with a GRDS campaign in Miami.

Results

  • ✓Cost per lead of ~CLP 1,400 (GRDS), ~1,700 (Fast no Junk) and ~1,900 (Club Gordos).
  • ✓At least 3 sales meetings per week per brand for 4 months: around 150 in total.
  • ✓According to the clients, each brand closed 2 franchises: about 6 in total, with tickets from CLP 75M to 150M.
  • ✓Miami did not work: a few leads came in, but no deals closed.

Approximate figures, rebuilt from each account’s reports. Amounts in Chilean pesos (CLP).

Takeaway

For high-ticket leads, qualifying in the form is worth more than a lower cost per lead: one pricier lead that books a meeting beats ten cheap ones. Miami taught another lesson: in a market where the brand is unknown, a lead form alone is not enough.
